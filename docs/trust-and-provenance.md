# Trust and provenance

| Concept | Meaning | Pilot status |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Present | An assertion exists with attribution and context | Implemented |
| Verified | Evidence has passed an identified check with a stated scope | Cryptographic admission validation exists; scientific status remains unverified |
| Trusted | A consumer or network grants a specific kind of reliance or privilege | No generalized scientific trust promotion |
| Disputed | An attributed challenge or conflicting assertion exists | CONTRADICTS relations; formal adjudication planned |
| Quarantined | Material is isolated from ordinary consumption pending review | Quarantine review planned |

A claim can be present and cryptographically valid without being scientifically correct. A receipt proves admission under the pilot, not truth. An asserted actor ID and a signing key do not establish a verified person, independent investigator or stable principal.

Contradictory knowledge need not be destroyed or overwritten. Preserve both assertions, their sources, context, methods, limitations and evidence. Relation authors remain accountable for their own assertions. A SUPERSEDES link is attributed history, not an automatic deletion instruction. Author retractions remain relevant even when records are still served.

Current public records separate `attribution`, `scientific_assessment`, `cryptographic_status`, `serving_state` and `provenance`. Preserve those distinctions when citing or transforming data. Inspect published edges and retractions; a curated view can omit other events and is not a complete challenge ledger.

Conceptual access tiers are public observer, authorized contributor and maintainer. Only anonymous observation and operator-managed pilot writes exist today. Future scoped sandbox/shared-intake permissions would govern resource access, not epistemic authority. Numeric trust scores, verified memberships and automatic promotion are not public protocol guarantees.

The [receipt protocol](receipts.md) reports canonicalization, hash, signature, bootstrap key, namespace, sequence and chain checks separately. A valid chain does not prove global completeness, prevent host forks, establish external time or authorize execution. The pilot has no retained authorization/policy digests or authenticated key-rotation history.
